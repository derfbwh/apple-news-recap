This Week in Apple: The Encryption Gap Closes

May 12, 2026

The big story this week isn’t just a software update; it’s the quiet closing of one of the most annoying security gaps in the mobile world. Apple released iOS 26.5 on Monday, and with it, the long-promised end-to-end encryption for RCS messaging has finally entered beta. For years, the "green bubble" wasn't just a social stigma—it was a security liability. Now, that’s starting to change. Meanwhile, the shadow of the upcoming leadership transition looms large. With John Ternus set to take the helm in September, every move Apple makes now is being viewed through the lens of the "Ternus Era."

The Green Bubble Gets a Shield

The release of iOS 26.5 marks a significant milestone in the history of the Messages app. While RCS support arrived in iOS 18, it lacked the one thing that makes iMessage indispensable: end-to-end encryption (E2EE). This week, that changed [1]. The beta rollout of E2EE for RCS means that your chats with Android users will finally be as secure as your chats with fellow iPhone users.

The Ternus Transition

We are now less than four months away from September 1, when Tim Cook officially becomes Executive Chairman and John Ternus takes over as CEO [3]. Ternus, a hardware veteran, is inheriting a company that is increasingly defined by its software and AI services. The recent focus on AI in the supply chain suggests that Ternus is looking at the entire machine that builds the products, not just the products themselves.
