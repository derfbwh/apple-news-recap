This Week in Apple: Accessibility Takes Center Stage as WWDC Looms

May 22, 2026

Accessibility has always been a core value at Apple, but this week, it became a central pillar of the company's AI strategy. In anticipation of Global Accessibility Awareness Day, Apple announced a suite of new features powered by Apple Intelligence that will fundamentally change how users with disabilities interact with their devices. From Eye Tracking on iPad to Music Haptics on iPhone, these aren't just incremental updates; they are transformative applications of machine learning. Meanwhile, the world of sports and professional production saw a milestone as Apple TV broadcast an MLS match shot entirely on iPhone 17 Pro. As WWDC 2026 (June 8-12) was officially confirmed, the week's theme was clear: Apple is using its most advanced technology to empower its most diverse users.

Accessibility as Innovation

The new Eye Tracking feature for iPad and iPhone [1] is a perfect example of how Apple uses its hardware-software integration to create magic. By using the front-facing camera and on-device AI, users can navigate their devices with just their eyes—no additional hardware required. It's a feature that will be life-changing for many and impressive for all.

The iPhone 17 Pro: A Professional Camera

Broadcasting a live MLS match using only iPhones [2] is a bold statement. It's not just about the resolution; it's about the dynamic range, the color science, and the ability to integrate mobile devices into a complex live production environment. It's a glimpse into the future of sports broadcasting.
