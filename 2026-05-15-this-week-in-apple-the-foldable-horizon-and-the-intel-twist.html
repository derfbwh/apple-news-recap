This Week in Apple: The Foldable Horizon and the Intel Twist

May 15, 2026

The air in Cupertino is getting thick with the scent of a transition. Not just the leadership change we’ve been tracking, but a fundamental shift in how Apple thinks about its hardware. This week, the rumors of a foldable "iPhone Ultra" moved from "maybe someday" to "get your wallet ready for 2026," while a surprising report about Intel suggests Apple is looking for more domestic flexibility in its silicon supply chain. Between professional tool acquisitions and the early start of the Back to School season, Apple is positioning itself for a very busy second half of the decade.

The Ultra Fold: Apple’s 2026 Gamble

For years, the foldable iPhone has been the 'fusion power' of the tech world—always five years away. But this week, the consensus has narrowed significantly. We are looking at a late 2026 launch for what is being called the iPhone Ultra [1]. The focus on a 'crease-free' design [3] suggests Apple is waiting until the technology is mature enough to meet its high standards.

The Intel Twist: Pragmatism Over Purity

The report that Apple is testing Intel's U.S. foundries [2] is a fascinating turn of events. After the successful 'Apple Silicon' transition, returning to Intel for any chip production seems counter-intuitive. However, it's a pragmatic move that allows Apple to diversify its supply chain and support domestic manufacturing without sacrificing its high-end performance goals.
